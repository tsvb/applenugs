The pack on the peg, as an icon

Tim picked the blister pack. Here it is six ways on a 1024 tile, each walked down to the 16 px Finder row, where most icons quietly turn into a smudge. The material is the theme's: card stock in navy, a punched hang slot, a clear bubble with a bright rim and one glare, a sale-yellow sticker in night ink. Pink only ever rides the plastic as a thin glint.

No wordmark, no band art, no flake. What sits inside the bubble is the real decision, because at 32 px the pack itself is just a lighter square and only the contents are left to tell it apart. Every icon is one hand-drawn SVG symbol, placed here under the iOS mask and inside the macOS tile; nothing is a bitmap.

My pick: the bubble

It is the only concept whose contents are already the app's own mark. The yellow disc with four stepping bars is the row sticker the app draws on whatever is playing, so the icon and the playing row say the same thing. It is also the only one that still has a shape at 16 px in the Finder once it gets the small-size tuning shown at right: a bright disc with bars cut into it, ringed by a pale rim, on navy. Nothing else on the Dock is that combination.

The full pack (Mint on card) is the better illustration of the theme, but at 32 px it is a pale square with a dot, and the hang slot that makes it a pack is the first thing to go. If it has to be the whole pack, the fix is to put the row sticker inside Mint on card's bubble rather than the picture, which is most of the way to the bubble anyway.

Before it goes into the asset catalog

  • Build it in Icon Composer as layers, not one flat bitmap: night and glow as the background, the sticker as the middle, the bubble's rim and film as the top. iOS 26 and macOS 26 add their own Liquid Glass highlight to the top layer, so the painted hot spot and glare streaks should come out or the plastic gets lit twice.
  • Hand-tune 16 and 32 px for the Mac: drop the flange and the streaks, thicken the rim, and grow the sticker to fill more of the bubble. The 16 px Finder row is really about 13 px of artwork.
  • Dark is nearly free: the art is already night, so the dark variant just makes the background transparent. Tinted keeps only luminance, which leaves the disc as the brightest shape with the bars cut out of it. That is the right thing to survive.
  • macOS 26 forces the squircle on Mac icons too, so nothing here can hang out of the tile, on either platform; the askew pack only works inside it.
