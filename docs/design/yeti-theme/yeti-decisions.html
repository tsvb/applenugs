1

What hangs on the peg on iPhone Home?

The first sheet put the blister pack at the top of iPhone Home, but labeled it “Newest show from artists you follow”, a feature the app doesn’t have. The card that does exist, Continue listening, is Mac-only today; iPhone Home starts at the greeting.

2

Show counts on the Artists list?

The first sheet printed “418 shows” beside each artist. The catalog list only gives a name and an id, so a count means asking nugs once per artist, and there are well over a thousand.

3

What does the line above the card say?

The first sheet wrote “Set 2, track 3 of 6”. The app writes “TRACK 3 OF 24”, counting the whole queue, because queued tracks don’t carry their set. The show page knows the sets, so carrying them into the queue is possible: real work, but small.

4

Does the Mac Home card get the bubble?

The first sheet kept the Mac’s only bubble for the dashboard miniplayer, so the Mac Home card is drawn flat. With the dashboard open, a bubbled card would put two bubbles of the same cover on screen at once.

P1

One phrase: “Sign out”

Today the connection screen says Sign Out and the account menu says Log Out, while sign-in says Sign in with nugs.net.

P2

Clearing the queue always asks first

Clearing empties the whole queue and stops playback. Only one of the three ways to do it asks first.

P3

The tab bar and sidebar stay the app’s

The first sheet drew different tabs and a Mac sidebar with a “Following” list. Following it would drop Videos from iPhone.

P4

The Signal section keeps its six rows

The first sheet showed five rows. The app’s six are fixed on purpose: when rows came and went with each track, the whole queue below jumped.

P5

The window buttons stay the system’s

The first sheet drew the red, yellow and green window buttons in frost gray. macOS doesn’t let an app recolor them; it would mean drawing fake ones.
